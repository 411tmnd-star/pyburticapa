Recipe, in order

Click the canvas to drop ink. Drag across it to pull a comb through the ink along your drag.

What each step is, on a real marbling tray
  1. Drop rows. Paint is spattered or dropped onto thickened water (size); each new drop pushes the old ones aside.
  2. Comb down. A rake with evenly spaced pins is pulled through, dragging stripes.
  3. Comb up. The rake goes back, offset half a pin, so stripes interlock.
  4. Waves. A side-to-side wiggle while pulling gives the feather or "bouquet" curve.
  5. Fine comb. A dense comb at right angles makes the fine nonpareil texture. Then paper is laid on the surface to lift the print.

The maths

Ink is stored as polygons. Two operations do everything:

OperationRule for each existing point pEffect
Drop at c, radius rp′ = c + (p − c) · √(1 + r² / |p − c|²)Pushes every edge outward; area is conserved, like ink on water
Tine along line L, shift zp′ = p + z · ud · M, where d = distance from p to L, M = line direction, u = 0.51/cDrags points near the line hardest; c sets how sharp the stroke is
Wavep′ = p + A · sin(2π x / λ) · ŷBends every stripe into the feather curve

Long, stretched edges are split as you go (the refine step) so curves stay smooth. This follows the published "mathematical marbling" approach by its authors; the code below is written from scratch.

Human makes, AI refines

  1. Make real prints. The look comes from the marbler's hand: palette, comb spacing, how far each pull goes. That's where the point of view lives. her words
  2. Lay out the wall. Put every print in one grid (the slide shows about 30 around three cover mock-ups). Pick by looking at all of them at once.
  3. Refine with AI. Use image editing to adjust colour balance and clean or move lines on the chosen print. She says AI was used "to fine-tune the details"; which tool is not stated. tool unknown
  4. Explore before committing. A Stripe Press designer describes the same split for another Stripe Press cover: AI used to test directions, while "the concept still comes from us. The final cover is still made by us." Designer Fund, 2026
