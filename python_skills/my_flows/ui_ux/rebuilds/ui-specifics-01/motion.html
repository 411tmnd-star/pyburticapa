What changed, and why it reads as "care"

It's a little jilted. You kind of want to get more of the scene, right?the speaker on version 1, ~15:30
  1. Speed. In version 1 the frame changes hard and continuously for about 4.3 s at the start. I measured frame-to-frame difference across your recording: it stays high from 17.7 s to 22.0 s. In the final, big changes come in short bursts of 1–1.5 s, and those bursts sit exactly on the wipes. measured from your recording
  2. Hidden transitions. The final never cuts. Every change of framing happens behind something in the foreground (trunk, leaf, trunk), so the eye never sees the camera "jump". my inference
  3. Consistent scale. Version 1 goes from an extreme close-up of the rug to a tiny fire in a wide shot. The final keeps the fire at a similar size until the hero hold. seen in frame
  4. Reveal order. Version 1 shows everything at once and then holds for 8 s. The final reveals one thing at a time (fire, tent, title, van), which is the "more of the scene" she asked for. my inference
  5. Depth. The final almost always has three planes: blurred foreground plants, the fire in the middle, trees behind. Parallax between them is what makes a miniature feel alive. my inference
  6. Motivated title. The title doesn't fade in. It's uncovered when the camera clears a trunk, so the move causes the reveal. seen in frame

Rebuild it in Blender

The same grammar, on an original night scene (a noodle cart under lanterns), built entirely from primitives by a script. Workbench "clay" renders, the equivalent of their grey C4D viewport. Play it and watch the beat list follow.

The clay render is not stored in the repo. Make it locally: python ../../motion/diorama_blockout.py --out ./clay --every 4, then python ../../motion/contact_sheet.py ./clay wall.png.

    Rendered in this workspace with Blender 5.2.2 as a Python module: 240 frames at 640 × 360, every 2nd frame of a 24 fps, 20 s timeline, played back at 12 fps. The two trunks and the leaf leave the set after they have wiped the lens, the way a film crew moves a flat between setups.

    Steps

    1. Block out the scene. Primitives only, real-world scale in metres, named groups (CAM, LIGHTS, GEO, SCENE). Put the wipe objects (trunks, a big leaf) on the camera path on purpose.
    2. Lock the camera before any styling. Key the whole 20 s move in clay. Use Bézier keys with auto-clamped handles so speed never spikes. Judge it on a contact sheet, not just in playback.
    3. Export guides for the AI. For each shot, render the first and last frame in clay, plus a depth or normal pass (Workbench, or EEVEE's Mist/Z pass).
    4. Explore style per element. In a node canvas (Figma Weave) or ComfyUI, feed a clay frame plus a style prompt into an image model, with ControlNet depth so the layout holds. Run one group per element (cart, lanterns, plants, ground, sky) and fan out about ten options each, the same structure as their board.
    5. Make one key frame per shot. Combine the winning element styles into one styled frame per shot, using the same seed and reference image across shots so materials match.
    6. Animate each shot. Image-to-video with a start frame and an end frame. Prompt the camera move in words that match the clay move. Open option: Wan 2.2 through ComfyUI-WanVideoWrapper; hosted options exist too.
    7. Review on a wall. Number every render v001, v002 … and tile them with contact_sheet.py. Change one thing per version, and log what you changed and why.
    8. Edit and title. Join the shots on the wipes so cuts are invisible, place the title where the camera uncovers it, and end on a loopable hold.

    Drive Blender with Claude

    The mcp-for-blender add-on (MIT, about 31k stars; formerly named blender-mcp) lets Claude run bpy code inside your open Blender. Work in phases and check a render after each one rather than trusting the text reply:

    PhasePrompt to ClaudeCheck
    1 Blockout"Create a 1:1 metre miniature: a cart 2 m wide at the origin, three lanterns over it, two stools in front, a ring of 30 cone pines 8 m out. Name objects by role. Grey materials only."Viewport screenshot from the front
    2 Wipes"Place two trunks and one large leaf so they pass between the camera path and the cart at 2–3 s, 5–6.5 s and 10–11 s."Frames 49, 140, 252
